# Writing Style

When writing or editing prose (documentation, user manuals, issue descriptions).

**Not in scope:** commit messages, the `rules/` instruction files themselves, and conversational replies in the chat
session. Draft prose presented in the chat for approval is in scope, since what governs is where the text ends up, not
where it was typed.

## Avoid AI-obvious punctuation

- No em dashes in prose. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses instead
- Go easy on semicolons in informal writing
- Do not overuse colons to introduce lists
- No one-word emphatic sentences as paragraph closers ("Good.", "Right.", "Exactly."). They look earned but are usually
  a tell

## Banned words and phrases

- Never use: "delve", "dive into", "leverage", "craft", "elevate", "streamline"
- Never use: "landscape", "paradigm", "journey", "robust", "comprehensive"
- Never use: "it's worth noting", "importantly", "notably"
- Never use dramatic transitions: "All of a sudden", "Suddenly", "Just like that". Use "Now" or nothing
- Avoid starting sentences with "This is where..." or "That's where..."

## Avoid AI-obvious structure

- Vary paragraph rhythm. Not every paragraph should follow setup, contrast, punchline
- Do not default to triplets ("fast, reliable, and maintainable")
- Avoid anaphoric triplets ("One X, one Y, and now Z"; "Not A. Not B. Just C.")
- Avoid mirrored sentence pairs ("X is not Y. X is Z.")
- Avoid time- or scale-contrast punchlines ("took five minutes. could have taken days.")
- Do not pair two metaphors against each other ("the X of A but the Y of B")
- Do not end every section with a grand summary paragraph. After the section's point is made, stop. A trailing 1-2
  sentence "verdict" paragraph is the AI tell. If the previous paragraph already said it, delete the closer
- Reread test: if the last paragraph of a section just restates the one before it more pithily, cut it

## Tone

- Write like a practitioner talking to other practitioners, not like a keynote speaker
- Leave some rough edges. Not every sentence needs to be polished
- Say things once. Do not repeat the same point in different words
- Avoid words that sound academic or editorial: "cognitively", "genuinely", "insidious",
  "calculus" (when used metaphorically), "value proposition"
- Keep metaphors plain. Prefer concrete descriptions over abstract imagery ("more code to review" over "larger surface
  area")
